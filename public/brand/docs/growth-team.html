Is this enough? The honest answer

Tools and agents are not your bottleneck. You can already make content, emails and reports faster than most startups. What's missing is proof (real learners saying it works), one channel that reliably brings sign-ups, and Google being able to read the site. A big team pointed at an unproven message just spends money faster.

"Invading the internet" means one thing in practice: a repeatable content machine (dozens of short videos a week), multiplied by other people's audiences (creators and affiliates). Duolingo's own team says a significant amount of its user growth comes from people seeing it on TikTok, and that the goal there is entertaining, not promoting.

The order that works: 1) make the site findable, 2) collect 20 real testimonials, 3) test 3 channels for 30 days each and keep the winner, 4) then hire humans to scale the winner. Hire in that order and every hire has a clear job and a number to move.

Where the Google Ads go

Start on Search, where people are already looking for exactly what you do. Only spend on broad formats (Display, App campaigns) once you know which users stay. Google's App campaigns optimise best when the app sends about 10+ target events a day, so wait for that volume.

  • Don't run Display or Performance Max first: they find cheap clicks, not learners.
  • Every ad group points at the page that answers that exact search. Never at the generic home page, except brand searches.
  • Ads for the Chrome extension link to the Chrome Web Store listing. As far as I know there's no ad slot inside the Web Store itself, so verify that with Google before relying on it.
  • Track conversions only after analytics consent (you already gate PostHog this way).

HelloTalk: how to do it properly

HelloTalk's rules list "advertising, marketing, promotional materials, spam" as not allowed in Moments, and posts are scanned by AI moderation. Spamming links will get accounts banned and hurt the brand. HelloTalk does run an ambassador programme for creators and sponsors YouTube creators.

Do

  • Real profiles that help other learners and post genuine progress
  • Partner with HelloTalk ambassadors who make content, with an affiliate code
  • Contact HelloTalk's partnerships team about a co-marketing idea (e.g. "practise words you met on HelloTalk in your favourite show")

Don't

  • Post links or promos in Moments
  • Mass-message users
  • Use bots or fake accounts

Our affiliate programme

  • Match the market: Lingopie pays 30% recurring, LingQ 20%
  • Run it with Stripe-connected affiliate software (Rewardful or Tapfiliate are common choices)
  • Give each creator a code + a landing page with their name

Pod101 (Innovative Language)

  • Step 1: join their affiliate programme and recommend their courses where it genuinely fits (e.g. after a learner finishes the French Fast Track)
  • Step 2: once you've sent them sales, pitch co-marketing with your numbers. Big brands answer people who already bring them revenue.

Creator outreach

  • Start with small creators (5k–100k followers): cheaper, closer to their audience, and more likely to reply
  • Offer: free Pro + affiliate share first; paid sponsorship once a creator converts
  • Always disclose: #ad or "sponsored"

Vibe coders

  • Your story ("built a full language app with Claude") interests AI and build-in-public audiences
  • Collaborate on "how I built it" videos and course modules; they get content, you get reach

Our own skills, written into the repo (.claude/skills/)

How LinguaScript talks: friendly, plain, the chameleon's voice. Hard rules: no invented numbers, no fake testimonials, red → orange → green → gold always means the same thing.
